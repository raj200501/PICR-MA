# Workshop Meta-Review

## Is this strong enough for a realistic NeurIPS workshop?

Yes, narrowly but credibly.

## Is the paper internally coherent?

Yes. The workshop version is substantially more coherent than the previous main-track framing. The title, abstract, results, discussion, and conclusion now all revolve around the same empirical point: policy-level permutation consistency lowers order sensitivity, but that diagnostic does not reliably predict held-out coordination quality.

## Is the main claim honest and interesting?

Yes. It is both honest and workshop-relevant. The strongest claim is no longer ``PICR-MA wins,'' but rather ``this symmetry bias has a real but environment-dependent effect, and the diagnostic proxy can mislead you.'' That is a worthwhile result for MARL and generalization audiences.

## Does the figure/table package look serious enough?

Yes. The package now includes:
- a clean method overview
- real MPE learning curves
- a compact ZSC summary figure
- a permutation-sensitivity figure
- a dedicated mismatch figure
- a main results table
- an ablation table
- an overhead figure
- appendix heatmaps and support tables

This is clearly stronger than a polished prototype with one bar chart.

## Would a workshop reviewer see value even though the main-track claim failed?

Yes. The workshop value is the controlled empirical story, not the claim of broad dominance. The paper would still benefit from a stronger learner and larger runs in a future revision, but it is no longer embarrassing to submit.
