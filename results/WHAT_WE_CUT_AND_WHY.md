# What We Cut And Why

## Claims that were weakened

- Removed:
  - any framing that PICR-MA is a new default method for MARL
- Replaced with:
  - a controlled-study framing about when policy-level permutation consistency helps and when it does not

Why:
- The 50k, 5-seed evidence does not support a universal win claim.

## Main-paper content moved to the appendix

- Full cross-play heatmaps
- Matrix-game summary
- Additional cross-family cross-play table
- Runtime table
- Permutation-sensitivity table
- Observation-slicing and asset/license detail

Why:
- These materials support the story but are not the fastest route to the main workshop insight.
- Moving them to the appendix made the main paper denser and more claim-driven.

## Results that were de-emphasized

- Matrix games as headline evidence
- Any presentation of lower JS as an automatic success signal
- Any reading of PICR-MA as clearly stronger than Deep Sets or Other-Play

Why:
- Matrix games are sanity checks, not the strongest evidence.
- Lower JS alone is not enough for the claim this paper now makes.
- The honest baseline story is mixed, and the workshop paper is stronger when it says so explicitly.

## Content that was added

- A new invariance-versus-coordination mismatch figure
- A compact main-paper ZSC summary figure
- A workshop-oriented ablation table
- A NeurIPS-2026-style `final.tex` and `final.pdf`

Why:
- These changes turn the mixed result into a coherent empirical lesson instead of a failed win-only story.
