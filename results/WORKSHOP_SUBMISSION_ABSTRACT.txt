Teammate features in decentralized MARL are often concatenated in an arbitrary fixed order, even when teammates are exchangeable. We study whether reducing this order sensitivity improves zero-shot coordination with unseen partners. Our testbed is PICR-MA, a lightweight policy-side regularizer that samples two independent permutations of teammate feature blocks and penalizes the symmetric KL divergence between the resulting action distributions, without changing the inference-time policy architecture. We evaluate PICR-MA against IPPO, Deep Sets, and Other-Play on PettingZoo MPE and matrix-game sanity checks using held-out partner cross-play, permutation sensitivity, and runtime diagnostics. In a 5-seed, 50k-step MPE cohort plus a 20k pilot sweep, PICR-MA consistently lowers permutation sensitivity and improves held-out coordination over IPPO on simple_adversary_v3, but underperforms IPPO, Deep Sets, and Other-Play on simple_spread_v3. Across runs, lower permutation sensitivity is not a reliable proxy for better coordination. The paper's contribution is therefore a focused empirical result: policy-level permutation consistency is a useful symmetry bias in some cooperative settings, but invariance diagnostics alone do not predict zero-shot coordination quality.
