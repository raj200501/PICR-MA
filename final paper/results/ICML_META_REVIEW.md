# ICML Meta-Review

The hardening pass substantially improved the state of the project. The branch now has a real 5-seed, 50k-step MPE cohort, repaired cross-play evaluation, a tuned PICR-MA main setting, a lambda sweep, a negative teammate-dropout ablation, updated paper text, and a reproducible artifact trail. That work is enough to judge the method seriously rather than as a scaffold.

The judgment is still negative. The best honest reading of the evidence is that PICR-MA is a mixed tradeoff method, not a robust improvement over strong baselines. In the main 50k cohort, PICR-MA helps on `simple_adversary_v3`: it improves held-out same-family cross-play over IPPO and Deep Sets while lowering permutation sensitivity. But it loses clearly on `simple_spread_v3`, where IPPO, Deep Sets, and Other-Play all outperform it on held-out same-family cross-play. This prevents the paper from supporting a strong environment-agnostic method claim.

The novelty is also moderate rather than high. Relative to Deep Sets, PIC, Other-Play, and broader consistency-regularization ideas, PICR-MA is best framed as a small policy-side regularization variant rather than a fundamentally new modeling direction. That novelty level would be acceptable only if the empirical story were clean and compelling. It is not. The lambda sweep makes the method look brittle: stronger lambda values reduce JS more aggressively but hurt `simple_spread_v3`, while the best cross-play lambda does not clearly improve the invariance metric over IPPO in the pilot setting.

The empirical scale is much better than the original Stage 1 toy runs but still only moderately credible by ICML standards. Five seeds and 50k steps per seed are respectable for a lean-safe local artifact, yet the spread curves are still moving late in training and the learner remains a lightweight NumPy implementation rather than a standard PyTorch PPO stack. Those two facts make it harder to argue that the observed pattern is final rather than an artifact of limited scale or implementation quality.

The manuscript presentation is now more honest and better aligned with the evidence, but it still does not clear a strong conference bar. It lacks the depth of appendix package, ablation figures, and broader empirical coverage that would be needed to offset the mixed main results. On balance, this is a careful and increasingly rigorous project, but not one I would currently recommend sending to ICML as a main-track paper.
